1. The short version

The ratings (pass, acc, tech) come from analyzing the map, and the acc rating is a prediction made by the Acc AI. Some maps end up paying players more than other maps of the same star rating, and some pay less. The acc reweighter is the only tool that uses scores to change ratings. It looks at how much each map actually pays the players who play it, and it multiplies the map's acc rating by a small factor (between ×0.885 and ×1.130) so that the map pays about as much as other maps of its kind.

It doesn't try to fix whole kinds of maps, like "pass-heavy maps pay too much". That job belongs to the pp formula and the balance tuner. The reweighter only fixes single maps that stand out from maps like them. A balance guard rejects any result that shifts the balance between kinds of maps.

Update: what changed on 22 Oct

Players reported two things: small buffs and nerfs that were unnecessary or too large, and maps that are clearly off not moving enough. Both were real. The cause was how the strength was chosen, not the worth metric.

The cause

Each map's target is part of the way to its kind's worth (Applied). But each pass picked its strength by the worth spread around 0, not by each map's distance to its own target. So strength 2.0 (the top of the range tried) won, and maps went much further than planned, more so the higher the stars. On top of that, every map with a gap moved by the same share of it, so small, noisy gaps moved about 2%, and the ±8% cap held back the maps furthest off.

2. The key idea: a map's worth

Everything the reweighter does is aimed at one number per map, its worth (BalanceTuner.Worth, with the PlayerAdjusted measure, which is the only measure the reweighter runs with).

log pp(score) = skill(player, half-year) + generosity(map) + distance(map stars − player level)

worth(map) = generosity(map) / reference(map's stars) − 1     (then the map-age effect is removed)
  • skill per half-year: players improve over time. With one skill value per player, maps ranked in 2026 looked 2.8% more generous, because their scores were set by players at their best.
  • distance: a map 1.5–2.5 stars under a player's level pays them about 4% more than a map at their level, and a map 8 stars under pays about 11% less. This term is fitted out so that a map's generosity doesn't depend on how far under its players it sits.
  • in-range scores only: scores on maps more than 0.5 stars above the player's level are ignored. A map that's too hard for a player says little about what it pays.
  • reference: what maps of the same stars average, from a local linear fit with a Gaussian kernel 0.25 stars wide (WorthReference). It isn't a per-range average, so a map that crosses a whole-star boundary isn't suddenly compared with a different group of maps.
  • map age: maps first played in 2022 looked about 3% more generous because they were grinded for longer. That effect is removed against maps first played around the same time.

A worth of 0 means the map pays what an average map of its stars pays. +0.10 means it pays 10% more to the same players. Maps with fewer than 20 usable scores have no worth, and the reweighter leaves them at ×1.

Why this measure

The older measure (Megametric75, how much a map counts in its players' profiles) reacted 3–5× as strongly as the acc rating did, and it inflated maps played by "survivors" with few high plays. The player-adjusted measure takes out who plays the map and where the map lands in their profiles, so what's left is how much it pays. 3% more pp on the tech 8+ maps moved their worth +1.9%, which is a stable and readable response.

3. The pipeline at a glance

  1. Reset: divide every acc rating by its stored multiplier, so each run starts from the raw AI ratings.
  2. Measure the start: re-score every score in memory and compute each map's worth and the balance stats.
  3. Star response: a small random probe measures how much worth moves with the multiplier, at the fit's base stars and at the real stars.
  4. Set targets: fit worth on the map's "kind". Each map's gap to its kind is shrunk within its noise, and its target is 70% of the way (in log) from its worth to its kind's.
  5. Phase 1, held out: 3 random splits. On each, fit on half of the players and measure on the other half, to find how far the data supports going.
  6. Phase 2, all players: fit on everyone up to the chosen pass and strength.
  7. Slope correction: a Newton search that puts the pass, tech and predicted-acc slopes back where they started.
  8. Worth limiter: stop the reweight from making any map an outlier (past ±0.03).
  9. Final balance guard at the real stars, with a fallback to lower strengths if it fails.
  10. Store, with each multiplier scaled by the star response (base over real), Leaderboards.AccReweight, the reweighted acc and SS/FS/SF acc ratings, and the stars. Write wwwroot/acc-reweight.json. Then re-score (RefreshEverything).

In FullPipeline it runs after RatingsRefresh.Overwrite → RefreshEverything and before the balance tuner (autoreweight: true). A full pipeline takes about 3.5 minutes in Release and 8–15 GB of RAM.

4. Step by step

4.1 Reset to the raw ratings

Run(): baseAcc[i] = lb.AccRating / AccReweightOf(lb)

The multiplier stored from the last run is divided out of the acc rating and the SS / FS / SF acc ratings. Each map's star range is also recomputed without it. Nothing builds on the previous run: each run is a fresh fit from the AI's ratings, so there's no drift that compounds from run to run.

4.2 What every map is aiming for (targets)

Path(): Residuals(Features(...)) · NoiseDeadZone = 2 · Applied = 0.7 · WorthLimit clamp

Before anything changes, worth is regressed (least squares) on the same features the balance tuner balances:

worth ≈ b₁·passShare + b₂·techShare + b₃·predictedAcc + b₄·OneSaber + Σ (one intercept per star range)
         (pp shares measured at the map's predicted acc)

The fitted value is what maps of this kind are worth. The residual is how far this particular map stands out from them. That gap is first shrunk within the map's measurement noise, then the target closes 70% of what's left, in log:

gap   = log((1 + worth) / (1 + kindFit))
noise = 0.28 / √(scores the worth rests on)
gap  ← gap × gap² / (gap² + (2·noise)²)               (the noise dead zone)
log(1 + target) = log(1 + worth) − 0.7·gap
target is then clamped to [min(worth, −0.03), max(worth, +0.03)]
Why

Kind fit, not 0: if the target were 0 for every map, the reweighter would also "fix" kinds of maps, which is the formula's and the balance tuner's job. Without this, the pass-share slope went from +0.03 to −0.37 in a single step. Dead zone: a gap within twice the noise barely moves (a median map with 880 scores has a noise of about 0.0094), while a large, well-measured gap moves almost fully. The step's old n/(n+40) moved every map with 800 scores 95% of the way, so 1,354 maps with gaps of only 0.01–0.03 moved about 2% each. With a dead zone of 1, 443 such maps still moved over 2%; with 2, 204. 70%, not all: the full fit (mean change 4.1%, 76 maps at ±15%) was too strong for players, who preferred the database without it. It was 50% while the strengths overshot the targets anyway; now that they don't (4.5), 50% only got maps 0.48–0.68× of the way to their kind, and 449 maps were left beyond ±0.05. The fraction is built into the fit itself, not applied afterwards: when a fraction of the full result was stored afterwards, it drifted more than the full result. Clamp: a map whose worth is already past ±0.03 isn't pushed any further out.

4.3 Which way, and how hard, to move each map (direction)

Path(): direction[i] = −weight × log((1+w)/(1+t)) / (Prior × relative × StarRelative)
direction = − log((1 + worth) / (1 + target)) / ( max(accShare, 0.3) × response )
  • Evidence: with the dead zone on, it's already in the target (4.2), so every map with a target gets the full step. With NoiseDeadZone = 0 the step is multiplied by n/(n+40) instead, n being how many scores the map's worth rests on.
  • Acc share: an acc multiplier moves a map's pp in proportion to its acc pp share. A pass-heavy or tech-heavy map needs a larger multiplier to move as much. The floor of 0.3 stops the multiplier from exploding on maps that are almost all pass or tech.
  • Response: in pass 1, the star response (4.4): how much maps of these stars move at the base stars, relative to the median map. From pass 2 on, how much this map's worth actually moved per unit of multiplier, relative to other maps of its acc share, kept within ×⅓ to ×3. At the real stars the median map's worth moved 0.6× its multiplier and the 90th percentile 0.98×; Godspeed ExpertPlus moved 1.3× because its stars jumped from 13.72 to 14.86 at ×1.072.

4.4 The star response

StarResponses(): StarResponseProbe = 0.03 · StarResponseBandwidth = 1 star

Before the fit, every map with a worth gets a random ×e±0.03 and everything is measured twice: at the base stars (what the fit sees) and at the real stars (what gets stored). Each map's worth change per unit of multiplier, over its acc share, is averaged over maps of nearby stars (Gaussian kernel, 1 star wide).

Two uses: pass 1's step is divided by the base stars' response relative to the median map's, and the multipliers to store are multiplied by base over real (kept within ×0.5 to ×2). So a 5-star map whose worth moves half as much once its stars follow the multiplier gets twice the multiplier the fit found.

Why

At the real stars, a map's stars move with its acc rating, so its reference moves with it and part of the change cancels out. The fit, at the base stars, didn't see that: once stored, low- and mid-star maps moved only about half as far as the fit meant. The per-map response can't catch it: it only counts from pass 2, and pass 1 makes most of the move.

4.5 Passes and strengths (a line search)

Passes = 3 · Strengths = {0.1, 0.2, 0.35, 0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3, 1.6, 2.0} · TargetMetric

In each pass, every strength × direction is tried as a candidate (4 at a time, each re-scoring every score in memory, about 150 MB each). The candidate whose worth lands closest to each map's own target (the rms of worth − target) wins, and the next pass restarts from there with a freshly measured worth.

Why each map's own target

Before 22 Oct, candidates were judged by the worth spread with the part of the gap each map keeps taken out. That left the kind's worth in, so the choice pulled every map towards 0, past the target of maps whose kind is worth more or less than average. Strength 2.0 always won, and maps went 0.83× of the way to their target at 0–5 stars but 2.14× at 11+ (all the way to their kind's worth and beyond). 89 of the 118 11+ star maps that went past 0 had their kind's worth on the other side.

Why not a fixed step

Worth isn't linear in the multiplier. A map moves within its players' sorted profiles, and every other map's worth shifts too, because worth is relative and players' weights change. Fixed steps oscillated. A line search measures the real effect instead of predicting it.

Fit at base stars (FitAtBaseStars = true): during the fit, each map keeps the stars and star range it had without reweighting. Then the reference a map is compared with doesn't move with its own multiplier, and worth follows the map's pp only. At the real stars, the slopes are far from linear, one Newton step overshot, almost every candidate failed the guard, and the held-out spread only reached 0.0385 (vs 0.0203).

4.6 Cleaning each candidate

Clean(): remove each star range's mean (log), then clamp to each map's bounds (MapLo / MapHi)
  • Mean 1 per star range (geometric): each range keeps its overall level and players' total pp keeps its scale. The reweighter moves maps within a range and never moves a whole range.
  • Bounds: Applied × ±log(1/(1−MaxChange)) = 0.7 × ±log(1/0.89), which gives ×0.922 to ×1.085, the same distance both ways in log. The older asymmetric bounds (×0.922 to ×1.072) held 182 maps at the buff cap that weren't noise (median 634 scores).
  • Large gaps (LargeGapMaxChange 0.16): a map whose gap (after the dead zone) is beyond 0.04 gets wider bounds, growing in proportion up to ×0.885 to ×1.130 at 0.08 and beyond. With the normal bounds only, maps beyond ±0.1 closed 40–50% of their gap, the others about 68%.

4.7 Slope correction

SlopeTerms() · MeasureJacobian() · CorrectSlopes() (Newton + Broyden)

Fixing single maps moves the balance between kinds by itself. On 21 Oct, the pass-share slope went from +0.006 to +0.007, +0.009 and +0.011 as the first pass's strength rose. None of the obvious explanations removed it, so three extra "kind" terms are added to every map:

logM += Σ_f β_f · u_f      u_f = −(feature_f − range mean) / within-range sd / max(accShare, 0.3)
f ∈ { pass share, tech share, predicted acc }

The β values are chosen so the three balance slopes go back to where they started. The Jacobian (how the slopes respond to each β) is measured once at the start. Every candidate in every pass gets one Newton step before it's judged, so the passes choose between already-corrected points. After phase 2, a Newton search with Broyden updates (up to 5 steps) gets the slopes within 0.0005. In the last run the β values were small (−0.0017, −0.0029 and +0.0028), so the passes had already done most of the work.

Why correct inside the passes

When the correction was applied only after phase 2, it raised the worth spread from 0.0248 to 0.0298, and the choice of point never saw that cost.

4.8 How far to go: held-out players

Phase 1: HeldOutSplits = 3, seeds 6969+split · Phase 2: every player

If the fit runs all the way to convergence, worth spread drops from 0.48 to 0.07, and much of that is fitting score noise. So the players are split randomly in two. Phase 1 fits on one half and measures, on the other half, how far each map's worth is from its target. The (pass, strength) with the lowest average held-out distance to the targets across 3 splits that also passes the guard on all of them is chosen. Phase 2 then fits on every player, up to that point.

Why 3 splits

With one split, the choice depended on which players happened to land in which half.

4.9 The balance guard

GuardMargins(): |start| + tolerance − |now| ≥ 0 for all 8

A point is only kept if none of the balance tuner's measures has moved further from 0 than at the start, beyond a tolerance:

  • Slopes (tolerance 0.004): pass share, tech share, acc share and predicted acc. Each is the slope of worth against that feature.
  • Gaps (tolerance 0.0075): top fifth vs bottom fifth by pass share, tech share and predicted acc, and OneSaber vs Standard.

Maps are classified by their shares without reweighting. Otherwise nerfing a map's acc would raise its pass share, and the slopes would end up measuring the reweighting itself.

Why those tolerances

The measure's own noise (rms over 10 splits) is about 0.0004 on the pass slope, 0.0010 on the tech slope and 0.0020 on the OneSaber gap. 0.004 / 0.0075 is about 4× the noisiest of them. 0.003 / 0.005 only allowed pass 1 at strength 1.0, and 0.002 / 0.004 allowed nothing. The tolerance is in effect a dial for "how much tilt we accept in exchange for fixing single maps".

4.10 The worth limiter

LimitWorth(): WorthLimit = 0.03, up to 8 rounds, at the real stars

With the kept multipliers (scaled by the star response) and the real stars, no map may end above max(its start worth, +0.03) or below min(its start worth, −0.03). If a map overshot because of its own multiplier, the multiplier is cut by the share of the move that overshot. If it was pushed by its neighbours (multiplier ×1, or the other way), it's moved against the overshoot. Then the other maps in its star range take back the mean, and everything is measured again.

Why

Without it, 66 maps already above +0.03 were buffed, and Godspeed ExpertPlus went from +0.010 to +0.107 (13.72 → 14.86 stars). A map whose stars jump climbs past its neighbours, so its worth moves more than its multiplier would suggest.

4.11 Final guard at the real stars, and the fallback

Finish() · FallbackStrengths · GuardMarginWarning = 0.25

The point that will actually be stored (after the limiter, at the real stars) has to pass the guard too. If it fails, the fit runs again at the lower strengths of the last pass, highest first, and the first one that passes is stored. If none passes, nothing changes. The margin of each guard measure is written out, with a warning when it's within 25% of its tolerance.

Why

Because the fit runs at the base stars, this was once the only check at the real stars, and it used to be skipped. On 22 Oct, Applied 0.45, 0.4 and 0.35 all failed it (pass slope +0.0080 to +0.0082 where 0.0079 was allowed) and were stored anyway. The fallback makes a change in balance weaken the reweight instead of throwing it away.

4.12 Storage and how it survives re-rating

Leaderboards.AccReweight (REAL; 0 or 1 = none) · RatingsRefresh.Overwrite / Refresh

The multiplier is stored per leaderboard and applied to the acc rating and to the SS / FS / SF acc ratings. Stars are recomputed (ReplayUtils.SetStars). When RatingsRefresh re-rates from RatingAPI, it re-applies the stored multiplier, so the reweight survives. Refresh(dbContext, false) clears it to get raw ratings. RatingAPI's own stars don't include the multiplier, because only portaBLe has it.

6. Upsides, and why

It fixes what analysis can't see

Why: the acc rating is a prediction (the Acc AI). Some maps really are easier or harder to acc than their patterns suggest, and only scores show that. The worth spread halves (0.0456 → 0.0231), maps more than ±5% off go from 926 to 79, and on the held-out players the distance to the targets falls 0.0289 → 0.0183.

Each map moves about as far as planned

Why: strengths are judged by each map's distance to its own target, and the star response makes low- and mid-star maps reach it once stored. Maps now go 0.80–1.18× of the way to their target across star ranges (was 0.83–2.14×), and only 12 maps are pushed past 0 by more than 0.01 (was 152).

It moves the right knob

Why: acc pp is about 80% of the top 1000's weighted pp, so the acc rating is the lever with the most reach. It's also the most uncertain rating. Pass and tech ratings, which mean "can you pass it" and "how technical is it", are left untouched.

It doesn't fight the balance tuner

Why: targets are the kind's worth, not 0, and the slope correction plus the balance guard keep the pass / tech / predicted-acc balance where the formula left it. Single-map errors and whole-kind errors stay separate jobs.

How far it goes is chosen by the data

Why: the held-out half of the players says where fitting stops helping and starts fitting noise. The fully converged fit (spread 0.07, from 0.48) was mostly noise. Averaging over 3 splits stops one lucky split from deciding, and the choice now lands inside the grid of strengths, not at its edge.

It leaves noise alone and moves clear outliers

Why: the noise dead zone shrinks gaps within twice a map's measurement noise to almost nothing, so only 204 maps with gaps of 0.01–0.03 move more than 2% (was 655), and 821 maps stay within ±0.5%. A map with few scores can't be swung by a handful of lucky plays, and maps without a worth stay ×1. Meanwhile maps beyond ±0.1 close 57% of their worth (was 44%), with wider bounds only for them.

Star ranges and the pp scale are preserved

Why: multipliers average 1 (in log) inside each star range. No range gets richer or poorer as a whole, and players' total pp stays on the same scale.

It doesn't create new outliers

Why: the worth limiter caps any map the reweight would push past ±0.03. That's the case of Godspeed ExpertPlus, whose stars jumped and whose worth reached +0.107.

Stateless and reversible

Why: every run starts again from the raw ratings, so errors don't build up across runs. AccReweight = 1 (or Refresh(…, false)) undoes it completely, and it survives re-rating on purpose.

It degrades gracefully, and it's auditable

Why: if the stored point fails the guard, the next lower strength is tried instead of dropping everything. acc-reweight.json records every round, every split, the guard margins, the rejected point and the per-map before/after worth.

7. Downsides, and why

It's one breath away from failing the guard

Why: the pass-share slope has 0.00013 of margin left, 3% of its tolerance (the acc share slope 23%). A new batch of maps, a constant change or new scores can easily push it over. The fallback would then store a weaker reweight (on 22 Oct with a 0.0037 tolerance, it fell back to strength 0.7: spread 0.0333 instead of 0.0211). The result isn't stable from run to run.

It tilts the balance, within what's allowed

Why: the pass-share slope doubles (+0.0037 → +0.0076) and the pass-share gap goes from +0.0040 to +0.0082. In practice, pass-heavy maps gain a little on acc-heavy ones. Any spread of multipliers, even a random one, does this: a buffed map climbs its players' profiles more than a nerfed one falls. The guard limits it but doesn't remove it.

Stars move more

Why: closing 70% of each gap, with the large gap bounds (×0.885 to ×1.130) and the star response scaling multipliers up to ×2, moves stars further: the largest change is +1.91 stars (+1.32 before the update). Wider large gap bounds (0.22, ×0.840 to ×1.190) closed the furthest maps 66% of the way instead of 57%, but stars jumped by up to +2.83, so they were not kept. 39 maps still sit at the ×1.130 cap and 9 at ×0.885.

Some maps get worse, through no fault of their own

Why: worth is relative. When neighbours move, a map's worth moves too. 587 maps ended further from 0 than they started, 18 of them by more than 0.01. Low-star Easy maps like Naughty little demon (−0.137 → −0.144) are at the buff cap and still dropped. Their worth barely responds to their own acc rating, so the limiter can't help them either.

Pass- and tech-heavy maps are under-corrected

Why: an acc multiplier moves a map's pp by roughly its acc share. With the 0.3 floor in the direction, maps with a lower acc share get a smaller step than they'd need. The star response corrects the step by stars, not by acc share, and the per-map response that would compensate only counts from pass 2, after pass 1 has made most of the move.

The fit and the stored result live in slightly different worlds

Why: the fit runs at the base stars, but the stored ratings change the stars. The star response makes each map's worth move as far as the fit meant, but not the balance: the real stars' slopes still drift from the fitted ones (pass share +0.0039 fitted, +0.0076 stored). Every attempt to close that gap (real-stars correction, drift compensation, frozen stars) made other things worse and is switched off (see tried).

Scores reflect player behaviour, not only map difficulty

Why: a map that's farmed (popular, good for a top play) collects better scores and looks generous. Half-year skill and the map-age correction remove the biggest effects, but not all of them. A buff can also attract more grinding, which raises the worth the next run sees. There's a feedback loop across runs, even though each run starts fresh.

New maps are treated differently

Why: a newly ranked map (fewer than 20 usable scores) stays at ×1 while its older neighbours are corrected, so its acc rating means something slightly different until the next run.

Stars no longer come from the map alone

Why: the stored stars include the multiplier, but RatingAPI's stars don't. Two systems can show different stars for the same map, and a map's rating can change without the map changing.

Complex, heavy, and tuned on one snapshot

Why: about 25 settings, nearly all chosen from runs on one Dev database around 21–22 Oct. They may not carry over as the database changes. Each candidate re-scores every score (about 150 MB each), and the full pipeline needs 8–15 GB of RAM. It's hard to explain to a player why a given map moved 4%.

8. Tried and switched off

RealStarsSlopeCorrection
run the slope search again at the real stars		 It got the stored slopes back (pass share +0.0077 → +0.0044), but it moved maps by their kind instead of their gap. Spread went 0.0211 → 0.0247, and 236 maps went past the worth limit. Pass-heavy, low-tech, high-acc maps were buffed to the cap (2cf6391 went to 10.85 stars), which players found too high.
DriftCompensationRounds
aim the fit at the start minus the real-stars drift		 The correction terms grew from about ±0.0015 to +0.04–0.07, and the real-stars slopes overshot about 3×. Spread 0.0211 → 0.0436. The terms move the stars, so the drift grows with them.
FrozenStarsRounds
refit with the stars frozen at the last fit's real ones		 The slopes came within 0.0008 of the start, but by moving maps that had no gap. Mean change went 3.3% → 5.1%, with 614 maps at the buff cap. It found a different, much stronger point.
Megametric75 as the worth It moved 3–5× as much as the acc rating and inflated "survivor" maps. Replaced by PlayerAdjusted.

9. Settings reference

SettingValueWhat it does
Applied0.7Fraction of each map's gap to its kind's worth the fit closes (in log). Was 0.5.
MaxChange0.11Bounds: Applied × ±log(1/(1−MaxChange)), so ×0.922 to ×1.085. Was 0.15 (same bounds with Applied 0.5).
LargeGapMaxChange0.16Wider bounds (×0.885 to ×1.130) for maps whose gap is beyond LargeGapFrom 0.04, fully from LargeGapFull 0.08.
TargetMetrictrueStrengths judged by each map's distance to its own target, not the spread around 0.
StarResponsetrueProbe of the response at the base and real stars; first step and stored multipliers scaled by it.
StarResponseProbe / StarResponseBandwidth0.03 / 1The probe's random ± log multiplier, and the Gaussian kernel width (stars) it's smoothed over.
NoiseDeadZone / NoisePerScore2 / 0.28Gaps shrunk by gap²/(gap² + (2·noise)²), noise = 0.28/√scores. Replaces EvidenceScores in the step.
Passes3Line-search passes. Each re-measures worth.
Strengths0.1 … 2.0Fractions of the direction tried in each pass.
EvidenceScores40Shrinkage n/(n+40) of the step, only when NoiseDeadZone is 0.
MinAccShare0.3Floor of the acc share the step is divided by.
HeldOutSplits3Random player splits in phase 1.
GuardTolerance / GapTolerance0.004 / 0.0075How much worse than the start the slopes / gaps may get.
GuardMarginWarning0.25Warn when a margin is under 25% of its tolerance.
FallbackStrengthstrueRetry lower strengths when the stored point fails the guard.
SlopeCorrectiontrueThe pass / tech / predicted-acc kind terms and their Newton search.
SlopeCorrectionTolerance0.0005How close to the start's slopes the search aims.
SlopeCorrectionStep / Max / Steps0.005 / 0.1 / 5Jacobian step, largest term, and most Newton steps.
FitAtBaseStarstrueFit with each map's stars without reweighting.
ResponseNoise / ResponseRange0.02 / 3When a map's measured response counts, and its allowed range (×⅓ to ×3).
WorthLimit / WorthLimitRounds0.03 / 8The worth limiter's bound and rounds.
CandidateParallelism4Candidates measured at once (about 150 MB each).
RealStarsSlopeCorrection, DriftCompensationRounds, FrozenStarsRoundsoffSee tried and switched off.

Any of them can be set for a single run without the website: dotnet run -c Release -- accreweight NoiseDeadZone=1 StarResponse=false [rescore] runs the reweighter alone on wwwroot/Database.db (rescore: RefreshEverything after it).
