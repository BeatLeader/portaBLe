How pp is calculated: a worked example

From the map file to a player's total: ratings, the map's own acc curve, the acc rating, stars and the pp formula. One real map from the database is followed through every step.

The example map

The chain at a glance

  1. Map ratings (from the map only): pass, tech and the predicted accuracy.
  2. The map's acc curve, shaped by the predicted accuracy and the map's sliders.
  3. Acc rating, read off the map's curve at its effective predicted accuracy.
  4. The three pp parts at a score's accuracy: pass pp, acc pp, tech pp.
  5. Stars: what a 96% score is worth, on the star scale.
  6. Score pp: the sum of the parts, the pp multiplier and the low star bonus, with modifiers.
  7. Player pp: the weighted sum of a player's scores.

Step by step

1. Map ratings

The Analyzer reads every swing of the map. The Acc AI (an ONNX model) predicts top players' accuracy on each note, and those predictions are combined into one number for the map.

pass = PASS_CALIBRATION_FACTOR (0.7721) × difficulty of the hardest 4–32 s windows
       OneSaber maps: × (1 − (1 − hands ratio) × 0.49)
tech = 20.611 × (average swing tech × (1 − 1.25408^−pass))^0.8015
Example

Pass 5.267, tech 3.157, predicted accuracy 97.995%.

Speed modifiers (SS / FS / SFS) get their own complete set: the Analyzer and the Acc AI are run again at that speed, giving their own pass, tech, predicted accuracy, acc rating and curve.

2. The map's acc curve

Every map gets its own acc curve, built from the base curve (1.094 at 96%, 2.700 at 99%, 7.424 at 100%):

  1. Shape by predicted accuracy: a table (CurveSettings.Table) picks the curve parameters from the predicted accuracy. The curve is base × modified / default, and never goes above the base curve.
  2. Slider tilt: the curve is multiplied by e0.2 × slider share × (acc − predicted acc) in %, within ±1.5% of the predicted accuracy. Slider-heavy maps get a steeper curve around their prediction: higher above it, lower under it.
  3. The curve is stored as sampled points (Leaderboard.Curve), which the pp formula interpolates.
Example

The map's curve is 1.074 at 96% (base 1.094) and 2.511 at 99% (base 2.700).

3. Acc rating

The predicted accuracy is first lowered where top players fall further behind the prediction, then the acc rating is read off the map's curve:

effective acc = predicted acc
              − 1.24 × (96.75% − predicted acc)      hard maps, floored at 95%
              − 0.03 × (hit distance − 0.62 m)       capped at 0.8 m, only under 97–97.5% predicted
              − 0.00214 × (pass − 14.52)             very high pass only

acc rating    = 13.5382 × map curve(effective acc + 0.19%)^−0.8222
Example

No correction applies: the predicted accuracy is above 96.75%, the hit distance is 0.508 m and the pass is low. The curve at 98.185% is 1.6743, so the acc rating is 13.5382 × 1.6743−0.8222 = 8.862.

Two details

The slider tilt isn't used for the acc rating. The acc rating reads the curve through Curve.PerMapValue, which applies the shape from the predicted-accuracy table without the tilt. Two maps with the same predicted accuracy, pass and hit distance get the same acc rating however many sliders they have. The tilt is only in the stored curve, which score pp and stars read. Stars can feel it: they read the curve at 96%, and on a slider-heavy map predicted at 97–98%, 96% is inside the tilt's ±1.5% range.

The acc reweighter's multiplier is portaBLe only. The acc reweighter is a portaBLe tool that uses scores. It stores one multiplier per map (Leaderboard.AccReweight), and when portaBLe rates maps it multiplies RatingAPI's acc ratings (base and SS / FS / SF) by it. It changes acc pp at every accuracy, and therefore stars. A missing value or 0 means ×1, as for this database. RatingAPI never sees these multipliers: a server calling RatingAPI directly gets the acc rating and stars without them.

4. The three pp parts

At a score's accuracy:

pass pp = 12.3 × e^(0.85966 × pass'^(1/2.62)) − 30     pass' = pass, but above 10.4 each point counts half; min 0
acc pp  = curve' × 21.584 × acc rating^1.2163
tech pp = e^(3.5 × acc) × 0.0891 × tech^1.2477

curve'  = curve(96%) × (curve(acc) / curve(96%))^power
power   = 1.0 up to 2★, linearly to 0.9 at 5★ and above

The power is anchored at 96%: there the ratio is 1, so a 96% score gets the same acc pp whatever the power. It only changes how acc pp grows above 96% and falls below it. Accuracy matters only in acc and tech pp; pass pp is the same at any accuracy.

5. Stars

Stars are the final pp of a 96% score without modifiers (step 6), on the star scale:

final pp of a 96% score = (pass + acc + tech pp at 96%) × 1.0833 × low star multiplier
stars = 1.2131 × (final pp of a 96% score / 1.0833 / 83.6925)^1.17507
  • The low star bonus is in the stars. The low star multiplier (step 6) depends on stars itself, so this is solved for the one consistent value.
  • The pp multiplier cancels out. It's included and divided back out, which is the same as leaving it out. That's on purpose: it only sets the overall pp level (fitted on top players' totals), so it can be retuned without moving any map's stars. Keeping it in would only change the constant (83.6925 × 1.0833 = 90.66 gives the same stars).
  • The curve power has no effect at 96% (step 4).
Example

At 96%: 32.2 + 329.2 + 10.8 = 372.2, so the final pp is 372.2 × 1.0833 × 1.000 = 403.2 (above 4.49★ there's no low star bonus). Stars: 1.2131 × (403.2 / 1.0833 / 83.6925)1.17507 = 1.2131 × (372.2 / 83.6925)1.17507 = 7.005.

6. Score pp

pp = (pass + acc + tech pp) × 1.0833 × low star multiplier
low star multiplier = 1 + 0.0321 × (4.49 − stars)^1.36     only under 4.49★; 1 above

There's no inflation power any more: pp is linear in the sum of the parts.

Example

On this map, acc pp is almost all of it. Pass-heavy maps have a much bigger flat pass part.

Modifiers:

  • Speed modifiers: SS / FS / SF use that speed's ratings, curve and stars instead.
  • Other modifiers multiply all three ratings by (1 + their values): NA −0.3, NB −0.2, NO −0.2, OP −0.5, and the rest 0.
  • NF gives 0pp.
  • Bonus pp is the score's pp minus what it would get without modifiers.

7. Player pp

A player's scores are sorted by pp, and the i-th best counts × 0.965i: the best × 1, the 2nd × 0.965, the 3rd × 0.931, and so on. The player's pp is the sum.

Where each part lives

  • Steps 1–3 are rating, in RatingAPI and its Analyzer.
  • Steps 4–6 are RatingAPI/Pp/PpFormula.cs and PpCalculator.cs, which the server can use without portaBLe.
  • Step 7, and the acc reweighter's multiplier, are portaBLe's (ScoresPlayersRefresh, AccReweighter).
