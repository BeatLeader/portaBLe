portaBLe Rebalancer Changelog

This branch rebuilds BeatLeader's rating system in portaBLe so that every rating change is fitted on, or checked against, real data: top players' replays, all ranked scores and fail attempts. Below is what changed and why.

The short version

Analyzer follows real play

Parity and swing directions are fitted on how top players actually cut. Pass is rated over time windows, and tech counts wrist work only.

A better acc rating

A retrained Acc AI, a curve per map, and corrections where players fall short of the prediction: the hardest maps, long hand travel, very high pass.

A balanced pp formula

One tunable class, retuned so no kind of map is worth more than others of the same stars. Ratings, stars and pp sit on prod's scale.

Balance tooling

An in-memory balance tuner checks every change, and an acc reweighter corrects outlier maps from scores, under guards.

Website

Top Scores and Top Acc pages, a curve explorer, a pass comparison page, map worth, a redesigned Leaderboards page and much faster graphs.

New terms explained

Dotted underlines elsewhere on the page link back here. The branch introduces several measures and tools. These are the ones used throughout the page and shown on the website.

Measuring balance

Worth
How much a map pays off for the players who play it, compared with the other maps of the same star rating. 0.00 is average for its stars, +0.27 is 27% more valuable than average, −0.20 is 20% less. It's the map's Megametric75 divided by the average Megametric75 of its star range, minus 1. A balanced system puts every map near 0. On the Leaderboard page it always comes from scores without modifiers, so "(no modifier)" appears when you pick SS, FS or SFS. On the Leaderboards list, +0.25 or more is tinted red (overpaying) and −0.25 or less blue (underpaying). It's blank when the map has too few qualifying scores or its star range has fewer than 5 maps.
Megametric75
How much a map counts in the profiles of the players who play it. For each score: the score's pp divided by that player's top pp, times the score's weight in their profile. A score that is one of a player's best plays and counts fully scores high. Averaged over the top third of the map's scores by weight, from players with 75 or more ranked plays (hence "75").
Star range (band)
Maps are grouped by whole stars: 0–1★, 1–2★, … 12–13★, and 13★+. Worth always compares a map with its own range, so a 12★ map isn't judged against 4★ maps.
Pass / acc / tech share
How a map's pp splits between its three parts, for a score at the map's predicted acc. A speed map might be 60% pass pp; a tech map has a large tech share. The three add up to 100%.
Share slope and gap
The balance tuner's main checks. A slope is how much worth changes per +10% of pass, acc or tech share: a positive pass share slope means pass-heavy maps are worth more than others of their stars. A gap is the average worth of the top 20% of maps minus the bottom 20% on that measure. 0 means balanced for both. Example: the pass share slope went 0.049 → 0.016, so pass-heavy maps are now barely favoured.
OneSaber gap
The average worth of OneSaber maps minus that of Standard maps with the same stars. Only 73 OneSaber maps are ranked, so this one is noisy.
Worth spread
The typical distance of a map's worth from its star range's average. Lower means more even. After the acc reweighter runs, it's low by construction, so it stops being a fair overall measure.

Rating a map

Predicted acc (Acc AI)
The accuracy a top player is expected to get on a map, predicted note by note from the map alone by a neural network trained on top replays. It drives the acc rating: the lower the predicted acc, the harder the map is to acc.
Effective acc
The predicted acc after the branch's corrections, which lower it on very hard maps, maps with long hand travel and maps with very high pass. The Acc AI ignores misses and bad cuts, and players lose more to those on such maps. Only the acc rating uses it; the shown predicted acc is unchanged.
Per-map acc curve
The curve that turns a score's accuracy into acc pp. Before, every map shared one curve; now each map gets its own, picked by its predicted acc. That stops easy-to-acc maps from paying a lot for tiny gains near 99%, and hard maps from paying too much just above the prediction. You can see any curve on the Curve Explorer page, and the map's own curve and pp table on its Leaderboard page.
Slider tilt
A tweak to the curve on maps with many sliders. The curve is unchanged at the predicted acc, a bit higher above it and a bit lower below it, more so the more sliders the map has. Slider-heavy maps were worth more than others of their stars, and their top scores usually sit about 0.5% under the prediction, so this trims what those scores pay while still rewarding beating the prediction.
Hit distance
The average distance, in meters, a hand travels between consecutive swings. The median map is about 0.62 m. Above that, the acc rating assumes players lose more accuracy.
Parity and resets
Parity is whether a swing is a forehand or backhand. A reset is two swings in a row with the same parity, so the player has to bring the saber back without hitting anything. The Analyzer now predicts these the way top players actually play them.
Pass windows and density
Pass is the difficulty of the hardest stretch of the map, measured over windows of 4, 8, 16 and 32 seconds. Density gives a stretch with more swings per second more weight, up to a cap, so a dense stream counts more than the same swings spread out.
Rating powers
Acc and tech pp take their rating to a power (acc1.2163, tech1.2477). The ratings were rescaled to look like prod's, which flattened their top end; the powers undo that inside the pp formula so hard acc and tech maps keep their pp.
Display curves (removed)
For a while the branch kept "internal" ratings and converted them to prod-like numbers only for display. Those conversions are now built into the formulas, so the rating you see is the one used for pp.
Low-star bonus
Extra pp on maps under about 4.5★: about +35% at 1★, fading to nothing at 4.5★. Top players push easy maps past 99% while stars are measured at 96%, so without it these maps paid too little.
Pass top compression
Above a pass rating of about 13.4, each extra pass point counts half in pass pp. Maps with unusually high pass were worth more than others of their stars.

Tools and pages

Balance tuner
A tool that re-scores every score in memory (a few seconds per run) and reports the slopes and gaps above, so a constant change can be checked before re-rating. Its results are on the Balance Tuning page.
Acc reweight
A per-map multiplier on the acc rating, chosen by the acc reweighter to move a map's worth towards what maps like it are worth. It averages 1 in each star range and stays within ±7.5%: only half of the fitted change is applied, since the full fit was too strong. 1 or blank means no change. It's the only rating change that uses scores, and it's kept when maps are re-rated.
Evidence weighting
Maps with few scores move less under the reweighter, because their worth is mostly noise. Newly ranked maps aren't moved.
Held-out players
The reweighter splits players into two halves. It fits on one half and checks on the other, and stops where the check stops improving. That keeps it from fitting noise.
Effective NJS
Note jump speed with the speed modifier applied (faster under SS / FS / SFS). The Leaderboard page shows it next to the base NJS when you pick a modifier.
Top Scores cache
A compact file with every score of a database (~56 MB for 4.4M scores), built after each refresh and uploaded with the database. The Top Scores page and the score graphs read it instead of querying the database, which is why they load in about a second.
Default database
MainDB: -1 in databases.json makes the last listed database the default, so adding a new database last makes it the default.

Analyzer: pass and tech ratings

Parity predicted with a cost model fitted on replays

What
ParityPredictor finds the cheapest forehand / backhand path through each hand's swings. The cost adds up comfort (distance from the parity's natural direction), rotation from the previous swing, a time-based reset cost (cheap after a long gap, very expensive on fast patterns), a table of which arrow pairs players reset on, a lighter reset cost after bombs, and a small backhand cost. The old bomb "parity flip" is gone, and a multi-note swing that contains an arrow follows the arrow.
Why
The weights were fitted on top players' replays (198 maps), and the pair table on 3.88M ranked swings. On maps the fit didn't see, 99.75% of swings match the players (96.1% on reset-heavy maps). Multi-note swings cut against their arrow matched players only 30% of the time.
Impact
Resets and their pass cost are predicted much more like real play. A variant fitted harder on wrist-roll maps was rejected because it missed resets on speed reset maps.

Swing directions follow how players cut

What
  • Single dots are pulled towards their parity's natural direction, plus a per-position arm-arc offset that moves entry and exit points but isn't wrist work.
  • Single arrows used to be bent fully towards the path from the previous swing. Now they keep 0.15 of that bend on the same row, and 0.3–0.85 on a row change, more for faster swings.
Why
Fitted on players' recorded cut directions. Dots: mean error 23.8° → 12°. Arrows (3.3M on 3886 maps): 15.9° → 10.2°, and rotation and angle strain follow the players' real wrist roll much better (R² 0.27 → 0.50 and 0.52 → 0.64). The full bend turned bottom-row streams into constant ~50° diagonals.
Impact
Tech no longer inflates on bottom-row streams and other same-row patterns.

Pass rated over time windows, with a capped density term

What
  • Windows: pass is the hardest stretch over 4, 8, 16 and 32-second windows instead of 8–128-swing windows. A map shorter than a window counts the rest as rest.
  • Density: each window's average is weighted by swing density (power 0.25), capped at the reference density.
  • Distance: DISTANCE_FALLOFF 2.668 → 1.0, so the distance between swings matters.
  • Crouch walls: ×2.0 instead of ×1.2.
  • Low-note nerf removed. It multiplied the acc rating by 0.6–1.0 on maps under 200 swings.
Why
  • Windows counted in swings let a few seconds of dense burst set very fast maps' pass. Seconds fit fail difficulty better (out-of-fold R² 0.628 → 0.664).
  • 16–128 s was tried first, but it overvalued long dense streams (Ascension to Heaven Expert) and undervalued peak-driven speed maps (The Purple Dimension). 4–32 s balanced better.
  • The uncapped density bonus also overvalued long dense streams.
  • Maps with longer hit distances were worth less than others of their stars.
  • Crouch walls fitted ~2.0 against fail attempts.
  • The low-note nerf was an earlier fix for low-star maps with few notes being worth too much, no longer needed with the rest of the changes.
Impact
Pass tracks fail difficulty and balances better. Shorter windows make a few seconds of burst count somewhat more again, a known trade-off.

Tech counts wrist work only, on prod's scale

What
Swing tech = angle strain + rotation; repositioning moved to pass. Tech = scaler × (average swing tech × pass factor)0.8015.
Why
Without repositioning, tech explained ~15% more of the acc players lose beyond the Acc AI's prediction. The power gives prod's tech distribution, whose top end is flatter.
Impact
Reset- and movement-heavy maps no longer get top tech ratings.

Reliability fixes

What
  • Deterministic ordering: ties in time are sorted by type, position and direction.
  • False resets: resets faster than the map's peak sustained EBPM aren't doubled.
  • Hit distance: a new Ratings.HitDistance, the mean distance between consecutive swings.
Why
The same map now always rates the same, EBPM is no longer inflated (Yggdrasil was the reported case), and the acc rating needs hit distance for its hand-travel correction.

Acc AI and acc rating (RatingAPI)

A retrained Acc AI

What
  • First retrain: 53 inputs per note instead of 49 (arc head / tail and chain head note types), trained on the top 50 replays of the ranked AccSaber, ScoreSaber and BeatLeader maps, with player-adjusted targets.
  • Deployed model: the same setup, plus training on the horizontal mirror of every map, and left-handed replays mirrored back so their notes line up.
  • Inference: the previous model is kept as model_sleep_bl_old.onnx; the layout is detected from the model's input size; speed modifiers pass 75% of their NJS change to the model; the last window is padded instead of dropped.
Why
  • The model couldn't tell arc and chain notes from normal ones.
  • A map's training target depended on who played it: grind maps get the very top players, which overweighted them. With the first retrain, the outlier spread in portaBLe went 0.584 → 0.531.
  • Mirror training cut map-level error on held-out maps from 0.1068 to 0.1015 points of acc, consistent over 5 seeds.
  • 4.8% of replays (left-handed) had been mostly dropped note by note.
  • The full NJS change under-predicted SFS acc, none made SFS scores worth about 6.5% less than the same players' no-mod scores, and 75% balanced both.
  • Training on speed-modifier replays, hard unranked maps, or extra weight on hard maps all made the model worse, so they were dropped.
Impact
Every map gets a different predicted acc. The deployed model's map-level error on 1037 held-out maps is 0.096 points of acc. It still rates maps from the map alone; scores only shaped the training targets.

A curve per map

What
Each map gets its own acc curve from its predicted acc, tilted on slider-heavy maps (higher above the prediction, lower below it). The acc rating reads the map's own curve. Curves are stored per leaderboard and per speed modifier, and can be regenerated without re-rating.
Why
  • 9–12★ maps paid too much for scores about 1% above the prediction (the 800pp+ plays).
  • True-acc and acc maps paid too much for tiny gains around 99%.
  • Slider-heavy maps were worth more than others of their stars (+0.48 for the top 3%), while their top scores sat as close to the prediction as elsewhere.
Impact
The 99%+ tail and the slider outliers are tamed. In simulation, scores above 800pp went from 246 to 31.

Corrections where players fall short of the prediction

What
The acc rating uses a lowered "effective" predicted acc:
  • Hard maps: below 96.75% predicted acc, lowered by 1.24 × the distance below, down to 95%.
  • Hand travel: 3% per meter of mean hit distance above 0.62 m, capped at 0.8 m, phased out between 97% and 97.5% predicted acc.
  • Very high pass: 0.214% per pass point above 14.52.
Acc rating = 13.5382 × Curve(effective acc + 0.0019)−0.8222, replacing 15.5 / Curve(predicted + 0.0022) × low-note nerf.
Why
All fitted on top players' scores after removing each player's own level. They fell further behind the prediction on the hardest maps (their pp stopped rising past ~12★) and on maps where misses and bad cuts cost more, which the Acc AI can't see because its training targets exclude misses. A tech term was rejected because tech pp already pays for it. The power gives prod's acc-rating distribution.
Impact
The Purple Dimension and similar maps are no longer under-rated, without overvaluing Godspeed.

API changes

What
The legacy star_rating and low_note_nerf fields are removed; slider_percentage, njs and hit distance are added. Rating can be limited to the requested modifiers and difficulty, and ONNX batches went from 4 to 512.
Why
portaBLe computes stars itself, the new fields feed the per-map curves and the acc corrections, and whole maps are predicted in a few calls instead of many.
Impact
Any other consumer of star_rating breaks.

pp formula, stars and scale

The pp formula in one tunable class

What
The pp formula moved from hard-coded ReplayUtils into PpFormula, which lives in RatingAPI (RatingAPI/Pp, with PpCalculator for score pp and stars) so the server can use it without portaBLe. Net changes:
  • Pass pp: PassScale 15.2 → 12.3, and each pass point above 13.4 counts half.
  • Acc and tech pp take the rating to a power: acc1.2163, tech1.2477.
  • Tech pp = e3.5 × acc × weight × tech1.2477; the exponent on accuracy went 1.9 → 3.5.
  • Low-star bonus: maps under ~4.5★ pay more (about +35% at 1★, fading to nothing at 4.5★).
  • No inflation: pp is the sum of pass, acc and tech pp times the pp multiplier (it was raised to the power 1.3 around 650, which made harder maps count disproportionately more).
  • Stars = 1.2131 × (final pp of a 96% score / 1.0786 / 83.69)1.1751: what a 96% is actually worth, with the map's own acc curve and the low-star bonus (the same stars as before the inflation was removed). pp multiplier 1.0786.
Why
  • Pass compression: maps with more pass than others of their stars were worth more above ~10★.
  • Low-star bonus: top players farm the easiest maps past 99% while stars are measured at 96%, so pp fell off steeply under 4★. An earlier soft floor on the acc rating squeezed those maps into 2.5–4★, so it was replaced.
  • Rating powers: with pp proportional to the prod-scaled ratings, acc-heavy maps lost pp against pass-heavy ones of the same stars, and no weight combination undid it.
  • Tech exponent 3.5: tech pp should go mostly to high-acc scores on tech maps, not almost as much to any pass. A 90% score now gets about 19% less tech pp than a 96% one (11% before), and tech pp is about twice as large at 96%. This departs from prod's 1.9.
Impact
Over the last development round the balance tuner's metrics improved: pass share slope 0.049 → 0.016, tech share gap 0.033 → 0.004.

Ratings and stars on prod's scale

What
Pass, tech, acc and stars match prod's distributions. Medians and 99th percentiles, now vs prod: stars 6.86 / 13.34 (6.97 / 13.19), pass 4.47 / 13.03 (4.44 / 13.39). The stored values are exactly what the formulas use.
Why
Comparing with prod was hard when the scales differed. An intermediate version used "display curves" over internal values; those were folded into the formulas themselves.
Impact
No hidden conversions. A server computing pp from RatingAPI's ratings must use the same rating powers.

Speed modifiers

What
Modifier multipliers apply to the ratings directly, and each modifier has its own curve and NJS shown on pages.
Impact
SS, FS and SFS scores use their own curves and ratings consistently.

Balance tooling

Balance tuner and Balance Tuning page

What
BalanceTuner re-scores every score in memory and measures each map's worth (Megametric75 relative to its star range). It reports slopes and gaps by pass, acc and tech share, predicted acc and OneSaber, suggests constants, and can sweep parts of the pp formula.
Why
So no kind of map is systematically worth more than others of the same stars.
Impact
Every constant change on the branch was checked with it. Its pass-calibration suggestion proved unreliable (often at the edge of its range).

Acc reweighter

What
AccReweighter moves each map's acc rating towards what its kind of map is worth:
  • Target fitted on pass / tech share, predicted acc, OneSaber and star band.
  • Evidence-weighted: maps with few scores move less, newly ranked maps stay ×1.
  • Line search over several passes, with how far to go chosen on held-out players.
  • Guards: balance metrics must hold, multipliers average 1 per star band and stay within ±15%. Only half of that change (in log) is applied, so ±7.5% at most: players found the full fit too strong.
  • Stored in Leaderboard.AccReweight and kept across re-rating. It replaces the old Autoreweight tools.
Why
The old tools were one-shot, unguarded and lost on re-rating. About half of each map's worth deviation reproduces across independent halves of the players (held-out spread 0.49 → 0.24); the other half is noise and is left alone. Simpler versions (correcting whole kinds of maps, fixed step sizes) broke the balance.
Impact
Outliers are corrected with balance kept: Ascension to Heaven Expert goes from +1.32 to −0.10 worth; 3585 maps move, 4.1% on average. It's the only rating change that uses scores.

Pipeline and project

What
  • Faster refresh: scores and players recompute in one pass (about 3× faster), Overwrite rates only the stored modifiers, and columns are checked before being added.
  • One pipeline: FullPipeline runs Overwrite → refresh → acc reweighter → refresh → balance tuner and comparisons. NJS and curves can be filled without re-rating.
  • New columns: Njs, HitDistance, AccReweight, Worth and per-modifier curves.
  • Project: Program.cs organized into sections, README and CHANGELOG.md added, a wider .gitignore, and UploadDatabaseAsync commented out by default.

Trade-offs

  • Fitted to this data: many constants (pair table, dot offsets, acc corrections, rating powers) are fitted on today's ranked set and replays, and should be refitted when the model or data change much.
  • Burst sensitivity: the 4–32 s windows bring back some sensitivity to short bursts.
  • Scores in the ratings: the acc reweighter uses scores by design, adds a second re-score to the pipeline, and makes worth spread unusable as an overall balance measure afterwards.
  • RatingAPI consumers: star_rating is gone, and pp computed from its ratings needs the new rating powers.
  • Small samples: OneSaber (73 maps) and the 13+★ band are small, so their metrics are noisy.
